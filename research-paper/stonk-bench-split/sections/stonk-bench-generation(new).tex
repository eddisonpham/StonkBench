\section{Market Generator Framework}
It is important to have standardized generation framework as
part of our efforts to create a unified benchmark \ref{contribute:3};
this section outines the details of the dataset used, preprocessing, and
 generation framework used by StonkBench for models to train on 
 historical market data.

\subsection{Dataset Specifications}
    The dataset contains daily close prices and trading volume for 20 U.S.
    large-cap stocks and 5 large index-tracking ETFs. The stock universe spans
    technology, financials, healthcare, consumer discretionary, industrials,
    communication services, consumer staples, energy, utilities, real estate,
    and materials. The ETF universe consists of broad-market and
    sector-focused funds. The dataset spans 15 years, from start of 2010
    to the start of 2025 from Yahoo Finance.

    For the generation framework, we focus on daily close-price time series.
    We represent the real dataset as
    $D\coloneq \{\mathbf{x}_i\}_{i=1}^T = (x_{i,1}, x_{i,2}, \ldots, x_{i,K})^T$.

\subsection{Data preprocessing}\label{subsec:data-preprocess}
    \subsubsection{Dataset Split and Transformation}
    Given our stock price dataset, we perform a chronological 
    seventy-ten-twenty partition to a training, validation and testing
    datatset $D=D^{train}, , D^{val}, D^{test}$, denoting concatenation. 
    This means $D^{train}$ spans the first 11.5 years (2010-mid2021), $D^{val}$, 
    $D^{test}$ spans the last 3 years (2022-2025) of our datasets.
    This deviates from the training scheme proposed by TSGBench \cite{Ang23}; we wish to 
    hold out our testing time-horizon from the training set completely
    to evaluate model generalization capabilities.
    
    \subsubsection{Log Return Transformation}
    We then transform the price time series for both sets into a log return time series, 
    which is a common practice in financial modeling to stabilize variance 
    and make the data more suitable for analysis. The return \( r_t \) at time \( t \) is calculated as:
    \begin{equation}
    r_t := \log\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}_t}{\mathbf{x}_{t-1}}\right) = \log(\mathbf{x}_t) - \log(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}),
    \end{equation}
    where \( x_t \) is the daily closing price at day \( t \). From now, on we refer to $D$ as the 
    log return time series dataset.
    
    \subsubsection{Sliding Window Transformation}\label{subsub:sliding-window}
    For $D^{train}$, given a pre-determined
    window length $L\in\mathbb{N}$, we applying a sliding window operation $W_L$ over the time
    series as follows.
    \begin{equation}
        W_L: \{\mathbf{x_i}\}_{i=1}^T \mapsto \{ (\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_{i+1},\dots, \mathbf{x}_{i+L}): 1\leq i \leq T-L\}
    \end{equation}
    For the purpose of research, we expirment our generation training with windown lenghts $L\in\{ 21, 126, ,252\}$ , represeneting
    general the number of trading days in a week, month and year, respectively
    \footnote{TSGBench determined the window length for deep learning training 
    and generation depending on the dataset by finding the maximum auto-correlation of that time
    series dataset \cite{Ang23}}.


\subsection{Market Generator Training and Generation}\label{subsec:generator-training}
    We provide a standard framework for training market generators over market data.
    Given a market generator $G_j, j\in\mathcal{J}$, where $\mathcal{J}$ denotes the index
    set of market generators to be trained and tested, 
    we train the model on the log return training dataset with the objective to 
    learn and generate sample $k$ of synthetic log return time series $D^{(n)}_{G_j} \sim G_j(D^{train})$ that 
    approximates the real log return time series $D^{train}$. For this paper
    we generate $N=1000$ samples from the market generators and evaluate the generator
    based on the samples, denoted as $\mathcal{D}_{G_j}=\{D_{G_j}^{(n)}\}_{n=1}^N$.

    \subsubsection{Non-deep Learning Model Generation}
    For the context of the paper, the parametric and bootstrap market generators consired
    can train on the full time series $D^{train}$. For a parametric generator $G_j$,
    it learns a set of parameters $\hat\theta$ from $D^{train}$ and generate 
    $D^{(n)}_{G_j} \sim G_j(\hat\theta\vert D^{train})$ such that $D^{(n)}_{G_j}$ and $D^{train}$ 
    span over the same time length T.

    For the block-boostrap generator $G_j$, it applies a random-sampling operation over the
    entire training data $D^{train}$ defined by the model to sample 
    $D^{(n)}_{G_j} \sim G_j(D^{train})$ such that $D^{(n)}_{G_j}$ and $D^{train}$ 
    span over the same time length T.

    \subsubsection{Deep Learning Model Generation}
    The deep learning frameworks considred in this paper (i.e. GAN, VAE, DDPM, signature-based)
    requires to be trained on the sliding window transformation $W_LD^{train}$ (see \ref{subsec:data-preprocess})
    such that these model learn to generate a window of lenght $L$ from $\mathbf{\mathit{p}}(W_LD^{train})$.
    We propose, for a given deep learning model $G_j$ that samples a window 
    $\tilde{x}_{G_j}^{(L)} \sim G_j(\tilde{x}^{(L)}\vert W_L D^{train})$, to sample $(T \% L)+1$ samples,
    where $\%$ denotes integer division,
    concatenate them sequentially, and trim out the right tail of the time series such that the resulting
    generated time series span the same time span $T$ as the trained time series; we repeat this process
    for $N=1000$ times to obtain $\mathcal{D}_{G_j}$. Formally:
    \begin{align}
        \left\{\tilde{x}_m: \tilde{x}_m=\left(\tilde{x}_{G_j}^{(L)}\right)_m\right\}_{m=1}^{(T\%L)+1} &\quad \tilde{x}_m \sim G_j(\tilde{x}^{(L)}\vert W_L D^{train}) \\
        \tilde{D}^{(n)}_{G_j} \coloneq \tilde{x}_1,\dots,\tilde{x}_{(T\%L)+1} =& (\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{T-1}, \mathbf{x}_T,\dots, \mathbf{x}_{T+L\mathrm{mod} T}) \\
        D_{G_j}^{(n)} =& (\mathbf{x}_1, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{T-1}, \mathbf{x}_T)\\
        \mathcal{D}_{G_j} \coloneq& \left\{D_{G_j}^{(n)}\right\}_{n=1}^N
    \end{align}
